BLB Suite Local Bible Reference Smoke Test

Independent manual test page for Double-click, Alt+B, Show on BLB, reference selection, context-menu behavior, and DOM-position handling. This page is deliberately not hosted on blueletterbible.org so the page under test cannot be confused with the destination site.

1. Natural prose — references embedded in paragraphs

Beginning, middle, and end

John 1:14 opens this paragraph. The Word became flesh and dwelt among us.

The Word became flesh and dwelt among us (John 1:14). For God so loved the world that he gave his only begotten Son (John 3:16), and Romans 5:8 also speaks of God's love.

The Bereans searched the Scriptures daily (Acts 17:11). Compare this with 2 Timothy 3:16-17, and remember that faith comes by hearing (Romans 10:17).

We walk by faith, not by sight (2 Corinthians 5:7), trusting the Lord with all our heart (Proverbs 3:5-6). Philippians 4:13

Multiple references in one paragraph

John 3:16 and John 3:36 should remain separate references. Matthew 18:20 and Romans 8:28 occur in the same sentence, while Ephesians 2:8-9 closes the paragraph.

6. Reference ambiguity and resolver boundaries

Standalone numbers that must not trigger: 1, 2, 16, 67, 150, 176.

Numbered sentences: 1. Install the extension. 2. Select text. 16 people attended. 67 items were listed. 150 pages were printed. 176 records were processed.

Book names with no reference: Genesis, Exodus, John, Romans, Acts, Ruth.

Potentially ambiguous prose: is, as, in, us, it, me, I. These are ordinary words, not Bible references.

Numeric-looking prose: Chapter 18, page 50, item 16, verse 176, section 67.

7. Boundary and maximum-value cases

Genesis 1:1 and Genesis 50 test the first and last chapter boundaries.

Psalm 150 and Psalm 150:6 test the Psalms upper chapter boundary.

Romans 16 and Romans 16:27 test the Romans upper chapter boundary.

Matthew 28 and Matthew 28:20 test a final Gospel chapter.

John 21 and John 21:25 test a final Gospel chapter and verse.

References with out-of-range chapter/verse numbers can be tested to confirm the established clamping behavior rather than rejection.

8. Selection tests

Select exactly John 3:16, then use Alt+B.

Select exactly Romans 8:28, then use Show on BLB.

Select John 3:16 and John 3:36 together and test MultiVerse behavior.

Select a reference plus surrounding words and confirm resolver behavior does not depend on exact historical wording.

Select text spanning inline elements and verify the reference is still detected.

9. Double-click targets

Double-click each of these independently: John 1:14, John 3:16, Genesis 50, Matthew 18, Ruth 1 1, Jn 3:16, 1 Jn 4:8, and 1 Thess 5:16-18.

Also double-click ordinary words immediately beside references to confirm the gesture does not invent a reference.

11. Case-sensitive and search-related text

LORD, Lord, lord, GOD, God, and god are intentionally different text cases.

Test case-sensitive search separately from Bible-reference gestures: b cs LORD.

Search-like prose: adoption to wit; injurious; Jesus; Scripture; Word.

12. Manual smoke-test checklist

  1. Double-click references at the beginning, middle, and end of paragraphs.
  2. Test Alt+B with an exact verse, chapter-only reference, range, and multiple references.
  3. Test Show on BLB from the local page.
  4. Test numbered books and aliases.
  5. Test punctuation and spaced references such as Ruth 1 1.
  6. Test references inside headings, lists, links, code, strong/em/span elements, and paragraph boundaries.
  7. Confirm ordinary numbers and ordinary words do not trigger.
  8. Confirm the destination opened by the extension is BLB; this local page is only the independent page under test.
  9. For MultiVerse, verify both the native BLB copy behavior and the Suite clickable-hyperlink enhancement.
  10. For KJV search, verify the single-result direct-verse path and the multi-reference two-tab handoff where applicable.

13. Final mixed paragraph

In ordinary prose, John 1:14 can appear near the beginning, while Romans 5:8 can appear in the middle of a sentence and Acts 17:11 can appear at the end. The same paragraph may contain John 3:16 and John 3:36, a range such as Psalm 23:1-6, a chapter-only reference such as Genesis 50, an alias such as Jn 3:16, a numbered book such as 2 Timothy 3:16-17, and punctuation around (Ephesians 2:8-9). This paragraph is intentionally long enough to exercise selection scanning across realistic surrounding text without relying on a specific Bible-site DOM.
